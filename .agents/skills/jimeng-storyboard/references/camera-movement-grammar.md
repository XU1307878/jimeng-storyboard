# Camera movement grammar

Use this reference to convert a dramatic intention into one precise, generatable camera move. Technique names are shorthand; the final prompt must describe the path, subject relationship and end state.

## 1. Movement sentence

Write every moving shot as:

`starting frame → visible or emotional trigger → camera path → speed and easing → relation maintained with subject → ending frame`

Example pattern:

> 起幅为人物腰部中景；她听见门外脚步后抬眼；摄影机沿视线方向缓慢推近，前两秒匀速、最后一秒减速；人物始终位于画面右侧三分线；落幅停在眼睛近景，门的虚影保留在左后景。

If the trigger, path or end state cannot be stated, prefer a locked-off shot.

## 2. Core movements and narrative use

### Push / dolly in

- Use for attention narrowing, realization, threat, intimacy or emotional pressure.
- State whether the camera physically advances or the lens zooms; do not use `推近` and `变焦放大` interchangeably.
- End on a new piece of readable information, not merely a larger face.

### Pull / dolly out

- Use for context reveal, isolation, aftermath, loss of control or scene closure.
- State what enters the frame during the retreat and why it changes the viewer's understanding.

### Pan / yaw

- Camera rotates left or right from a fixed position.
- Use to follow a gaze, scan a space, connect two subjects or delay a reveal.
- Give start target, rotation direction and final target. Avoid an unmotivated panoramic sweep.

### Truck / lateral move

- Camera translates left or right while its viewing direction remains broadly stable.
- Use for parallel action, spatial comparison, passing foreground layers or revealing something behind an obstruction.
- Lock the subject's screen direction and specify whether framing stays constant or changes.

### Follow / tracking

- Camera moves with the subject to create participation, pursuit or continuous geography.
- State lead, side or rear follow; fixed approximate distance; subject path; and final separation.
- Let footfall, body acceleration and turns motivate camera acceleration and inertia.

### Pedestal / crane up-down

- Camera position rises or falls without merely tilting.
- Use for hierarchy, discovery across vertical layers, descent into danger or release from confinement.
- Distinguish physical rise/fall from tilt and state the newly revealed vertical layer.

### Tilt / pitch

- Camera rotates up or down from a fixed position.
- Use to reveal scale, follow standing/falling action, connect prop to face, or reveal authority above.
- Give start and end targets; preserve the subject's horizontal screen position where possible.

### Orbit / arc

- Camera travels around a subject or relationship axis.
- Use for a power shift, mutual attraction, entrapment, ritual or transformed spatial understanding.
- Specify center, angle, direction and stopping side. Prefer 15–45 degrees for dialogue; use larger arcs only when identity and geometry are stable.
- Avoid large orbit moves in face-critical close-ups.

### Roll / rotational horizon

- Camera rotates around the lens axis and tilts the horizon.
- Reserve for disorientation, intoxication, supernatural inversion or loss of balance.
- State maximum roll and recovery. Do not use as a generic intensity effect.

### Locked-off

- Use for authority, deadpan comedy, surveillance, helpless observation, tension before an intrusion or a performance that should dominate the frame.
- State which subjects and environmental details still move so the model does not produce a static image.

### Handheld

- Use for urgency, instability, documentary immediacy or close subjective pressure.
- Define amplitude, rhythm and cause: restrained breathing drift, walking impact, running pursuit or collision aftershock.
- `手持` does not mean random motion. Keep the subject readable and avoid continuous high-frequency shake.

## 3. Compound movement

Combine movements only when one physical or dramatic cause connects them:

- push + tilt up: approach a subject and reveal authority above;
- follow + lateral shift: track a walker, then expose the person behind them;
- crane down + pull back: descend into a group while revealing its formation;
- orbit + push: intensify a relationship while shifting power, used sparingly.

Rules:

- one dominant movement and at most one subordinate axis per beat;
- describe the order, transition point and easing;
- never write simultaneous `推近又拉远`, `快速又缓慢`, or conflicting left/right paths unless the reversal is a visible scripted event;
- if a compound move serves two unrelated story purposes, split the shot.

## 4. Speed and easing

Tie speed to story rhythm:

- very slow: dread, observation, suppressed emotion;
- slow: attention, intimacy, controlled revelation;
- steady: geography, walking dialogue, process;
- fast: pursuit, surprise, decisive action;
- whip: abrupt redirection or transition, requiring a readable settled frame before and after.

State acceleration when useful: ease in, constant speed, accelerate with the subject, impact jolt, then settle. Avoid false measurements for ordinary acting.

## 5. Vertical 9:16 adaptation

- Use push/pull to change emotional distance without losing vertical body hierarchy.
- Use tilt and pedestal to connect low foreground props, mid-frame faces and high background threats.
- For lateral moves, keep travel short or use strong foreground layers; long horizontal travel wastes the narrow frame.
- In follow shots, declare whether the character stays center, upper third or lower-middle, and reserve vertical headroom for the destination or threat.
- Orbit angles should usually be smaller than in 16:9 because the background changes more abruptly in a narrow frame.

## 6. AI-video stability locks

- Restate starting pose, hand contact, gaze, screen position and key-light direction before movement.
- Give one stable subject anchor throughout the move.
- End on a measurable pose, prop state, gaze target, obstruction or settled frame.
- Keep camera movement separate from character movement: say what the person does and what the camera does.
- Preserve axis side, motion direction, focal length and key-light direction.
- For 20–30 second clips, divide a long move into motivated phases with settled checkpoints; do not ask for continuous acceleration for the entire duration.
- When faces, hands, crowds, animals or complex props are high risk, shorten the path or split the shot rather than adding more negative terms.

## 7. Selection test

Before using a move, answer:

1. What new information or feeling appears because the camera moves?
2. Why does the movement begin at this exact moment?
3. What relationship must remain stable during the path?
4. Where does the camera stop, and what can the next shot inherit?

If the answer is only `更有电影感`, choose a more motivated movement or keep the camera still.
