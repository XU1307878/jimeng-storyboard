# Director Core

## Dramatic reading

Before choosing shots, establish:

- whose experience organizes the scene;
- what that character wants now;
- what blocks the objective;
- what the audience knows before and after each beat;
- the visible or audible trigger for each emotional change;
- the decisive reveal, reversal or choice;
- the state that must survive into the following scene.

Do not confuse atmosphere with story progress. A shot earns its place by adding action, emotion, information, spatial understanding or a sound transition.

Before choosing a visible convention, name its dramatic job. Translate that job into one dominant channel—space, composition, shape, editing, time or sound—and use the least elaborate device that makes the change legible. A convention is not justified merely because it looks cinematic.

## Opening design

For short-form work, place a visible question, anomaly, threat, contradiction or emotional wound as early as the source permits. Choose among:

- conflict already in progress;
- a normal action disrupted by one abnormal detail;
- a consequential object or line shown before its explanation;
- an emotionally charged outcome followed by a clearly marked return to earlier time;
- direct first-person testimony when the script's viewpoint supports it.

Do not invent a stronger incident. Reorder existing material only when adaptation or viral-opening optimization is requested, the later context will be restored, and the temporal transition is legible. An atmospheric establishing shot is valid when place itself supplies the question or when the requested format is not retention-driven.

## Textual production locks

Lock only facts supported by the script, user direction or references:

- character identity and current costume state;
- location zones, entrances, exits and obstacles;
- critical props, owner, hand/contact state and exact resting place;
- time, weather and motivated light sources;
- relationship axis, camera side, screen direction and eyelines.

Do not fabricate hair, eye color, costume, lens package or visual medium to fill a blank. Mark the smallest necessary assumption as provisional.

## Shot progression

Choose the smallest useful progression. Possible functions include:

1. context or threat geometry;
2. blocking or core action;
3. reaction and decision;
4. decisive detail or proof;
5. consequence, release or hook.

This is a menu, not a mandatory five-shot sequence. Compress, reorder or omit functions based on the script. Use one dominant camera behavior per shot and define its motivation, starting frame, trigger, path, maintained relation and ending frame.

When a beat can be carried without dialogue, prefer visible or audible evidence: a changed spatial distance, broken composition, redirected gaze, prop state, sound reaction, time cue or meaningful juxtaposition. Do not remove dialogue mechanically; preserve it when it carries information or character voice that the image cannot replace.

## Three-layer performance

For every visible principal performance, specify:

- `动作`: posture, weight transfer, hand contact, body action and end pose;
- `表情`: the readable public emotion or social mask;
- `微表情`: two to four decisive cues from gaze, eyelids, brows, mouth, jaw, breath or grip pressure.

Derive the transition as:

`trigger → appraisal → immediate objective → tactic/social mask → action + expression + micro-expression + voice → residual state`

Keep appraisal and objective in director reasoning; put only visible or audible evidence into the production payload. Prefer sequential leakage: eyes react before the head, breath changes before speech, and a hand may betray what the face tries to hide. Background characters receive restrained two-layer reactions and must not steal focus.

## Action physics

For forceful action, use `preparation → contact/impact → aftereffect` and state:

- weight and balance;
- contact point and resistance;
- effect on body, prop or environment;
- recoverable end pose and new traces.

For delicate action, keep continuous small movements and explicit hand-object contact. Do not overload one generation clip with multiple unrelated action objectives.

## Lighting and sound

Encode light as:

`source + screen position/height + direction + softness + color + subject effect + background relationship`

Keep source side, shadow direction and exposure stable across reverse angles. Change light only through an on-screen source event, time shift or motivated movement.

Build sound from:

- ambience that locates the scene;
- physical action feedback;
- dialogue, breath, offscreen cue or silence that changes the beat.

Silence is a designed contrast, not an instruction to remove all sound indiscriminately. Do not add background music unless the user or platform workflow calls for it.

## Continuity relay

For each boundary, record the outgoing and incoming state of:

- body pose and movement direction;
- gaze target and screen direction;
- prop hand/contact and position;
- key-light direction and intensity;
- environmental motion;
- dialogue breath, ambience and sound cue.

Split or redesign any shot whose end state cannot plausibly become the next shot's entry state.

## Quality check

- The shot language expresses this script rather than a generic genre trailer.
- Every conspicuous convention has a stated dramatic job and a visible or audible payoff.
- Every emotional reaction has a prior trigger.
- Camera movement has one narrative purpose and does not conflict with blocking.
- The 180-degree axis and eyelines are preserved or deliberately re-established.
- Props do not teleport, duplicate or change hands invisibly.
- Lighting does not follow the camera during reverse angles.
- No medium, aspect ratio or technology was invented.
- The final frame contains a measurable state for the next shot.
